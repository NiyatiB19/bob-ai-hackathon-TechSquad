# Problem Statement

## Background
Global logistics networks connect manufacturers, freight forwarders, fleet dispatchers, and warehouse operators across international trade corridors. However, modern supply chains are highly vulnerable to unpredictable external disruptions—such as severe weather phenomena (blizzards, hurricanes), port labor strikes, geopolitical conflicts, and canal blockages—which can materialize within hours.

## The Problem
When a major disruption strikes a primary trade corridor, hundreds of active shipments are stranded or immobilized simultaneously. Primary transit routes become severely bottlenecked, alternative channels suffer extreme capacity overloads, and demurrage costs compound exponentially. Concurrently, regional fleet assets (trucks, refrigerated containers, vessels) often sit idle in uninvolved locations due to a lack of real-time operational visibility. Cold-chain cargo—such as mRNA vaccines, biopharmaceuticals, and fresh produce—is especially at risk, as unmonitored thermal excursions spoil sensitive loads long before delivery.

## Who is Affected
Logistics operations managers, fleet dispatchers, freight forwarders, supply chain directors, and cold-chain quality control assurance officers managing complex, time-sensitive freight.

## Why It Matters
Unmitigated disruptions cause severe economic damage: millions of dollars in spoiled pharmaceutical/food cargo, contractual late-delivery penalties, wasted fuel, inflated freight spot-market rates, and severe supply shortages for downstream consumers and health services.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short
Current monitoring relies on fragmented tracking portals, manual weather checks, disconnected IoT telematics feeds, and manual email/phone coordination. Operators are overwhelmed by raw data, react too late after cargo is already spoiled, and lack centralized AI decision support to dynamically evaluate rerouting options and redeploy idle fleet assets in real time.
