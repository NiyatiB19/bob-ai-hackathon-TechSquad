# Detailed Problem Statement — L2: Supply Chain Disruption Assistant & Fleet Utilisation Optimizer

## 1. Target Stakeholders & Experience
Modern supply chains involve complex networks of logistics providers, freight forwarders, fleet dispatchers, and warehouse operators. When unexpected disruptions occur, these stakeholders are tasked with protecting cargo integrity, maintaining delivery timelines, and controlling operational costs across hundreds of concurrent shipments.

## 2. Dynamics of Supply Chain Disruptions
Major disruptions—such as severe weather phenomena (hurricanes, blizzards), port labor strikes, geopolitical conflicts, and canal blockages—can materialize rapidly. These external shocks disrupt primary transit corridors, freeze maritime and ground transit routes, and invalidate established shipping schedules within hours.

## 3. Impact on Active Shipments
When a major disruption occurs:
- Hundreds of active transit legs are immediately immobilized or stranded in high-risk zones.
- Secondary and tertiary transit channels quickly become bottlenecked as carriers attempt ad-hoc rerouting without central orchestration.
- Demurrage charges, transit delays, and fuel penalties compound rapidly across affected supply chains.

## 4. Fleet Utilization & Asset Misallocation
Simultaneously, logistics operations suffer from severe asset inefficiency:
- Fleet assets (heavy duty trucks, refrigerated containers, chassis, and regional vessels) often sit idle in uninvolved freight terminals.
- Meanwhile, neighboring routes experience acute capacity shortages and container deficits.
- Without cross-network visibility, dispatchers cannot dynamically identify and redeploy these under-utilized assets to relieve overloaded corridors.

## 5. Cold-Chain Vulnerabilities & Late Breach Detection
Cold-chain shipments—such as biological vaccines, temperature-sensitive pharmaceuticals, fresh produce, and frozen goods—are uniquely vulnerable to disruption-induced delays:
- Extended delays or malfunctioning refrigeration units lead to thermal excursions (temperature spikes or drops beyond safe thresholds).
- Currently, temperature breaches are frequently discovered only at physical destination delivery, resulting in total cargo loss, liability disputes, and compromised public safety.

## 6. Challenges of Manual Monitoring
Logistics teams currently rely on fragmented tracking portals, manual weather monitoring, disconnected IoT telemetry dashboards, and manual phone/email outreach:
- **Fragmented Context:** Information is siloed across separate carrier platforms, telematics providers, and sensor feeds.
- **High Data Volume:** Operators are overwhelmed by raw data without clear prioritization of high-risk shipments.
- **Reactive Response:** Remedial actions are taken long after cargo has already spoiled or missed critical connection windows.

## 7. The Necessity of Timely Decision Support
Proactive risk mitigation requires immediate, automated intelligence. Rapid detection of disruptions, combined with real-time risk scoring and recommended rerouting, enables logistics operators to intervene before shipments fail, asset waste accumulates, or cold-chain integrity is irrevocably breached.

## 8. What SupplyGuard AI Intends to Solve
SupplyGuard AI aims to establish an intelligent decision-support system that:
- Continuously correlates active shipment vectors against active disruption events.
- Recommends optimal alternative transit corridors and carrier deployments.
- Automatically identifies idle fleet assets and proposes redeployment strategies.
- Real-time monitors cold-chain IoT telemetry, detecting and classifying temperature excursions before cargo delivery.
- Integrates IBM Bob as a conversational AI decision-support assistant to empower operators with rapid, prioritized recommendations.
